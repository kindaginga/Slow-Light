It looks at the image before it touches it.

Most motion tools ask you to paint where things should move. Slow Light already knows what a galaxy is made of. It finds every point source above the noise, every warm star-forming region, and the diffuse gas between them, then animates each in the way it would actually behave.

How it works

Everything happens in your browser, on your graphics chip. There is no server and no upload.

  1. Find

    A top-hat filter separates compact sources from the smooth background. Anything above the measured noise floor becomes a star. Red excess marks the star-forming knots.

  2. Warp

    A single shader rotates the galaxy with angular speed falling off with radius, then adds a slow drift to the gas so dust lanes breathe rather than sit still.

  3. Glow

    Stars and knots are drawn as soft additive light, rising and dipping around their original brightness.

  4. Loop

    Every motion is an exact number of cycles over the clip, so the last frame flows into the first with no crossfade and no seam.

Your image stays yours.

No downscaling

Preview at screen size, export at the image's native size. A 3600×2702 source produces a 3600×2702 loop.

No upload

Analysis and rendering run on the device. Slow Light works offline once it has loaded, and can be installed like an app.
